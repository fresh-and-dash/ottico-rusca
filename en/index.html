The method

12345

Every visit follows the same path, whether it is a first pair of glasses, a complex prescription or contact lenses.

  1. 1

    The conversation

    Before any measurement, a conversation: how you use your eyes during the day, at what distance you work, how much time you spend in front of a screen, whether you drive at night, whether you have worn progressive lenses or contact lenses before. The right correction depends on these answers, and on others besides.

  2. 2

    The eye examination

    A complete refraction, not a quick test. Everything needed to define the exact correction is measured, calmly enough that nothing is left to approximation. How the eye examination works

  3. 3

    Choosing lenses and frame

    Lenses are chosen according to the prescription and to real use; the frame according to the face and to the correction it will have to carry. It is the opposite of the rule in many shops: eyesight first, then looks. Done in the right order, the two go together.

  4. 4

    Fitting and adjustment

    The master optician personally carries out centring, fitting and final adjustment. An excellent lens fitted a few millimetres off stops being an excellent lens.

  5. 5

    The follow-up

    After a few weeks we check that you have fully adapted and that everything works as it should. For contact lenses, check-ups are part of the fitting and are scheduled from the very start.

The right frame exists. But it is chosen after the measurement.

Every frame places constraints on the lenses: size, curvature, distance from the eyes. With a strong prescription, a large lens becomes thick and heavy; with a progressive, too little height compromises the reading area. That is why, here, the frame is chosen together with the optician, after the examination and not before.

Born in Lugano, he first obtained the Ticino cantonal diploma and then, in 1986, the Swiss federal optician’s diploma (master optician) at the Swiss Higher School of Optics in Olten. He has run his own practice in Lugano-Crocifisso ever since. For many years he combined practice with teaching optics at the Centro Professionale Tecnico: those who teach a profession know it inside out.

The practice grew from a simple conviction, formed over decades of practice: eyesight is measured with patience and corrected with precision, and neither can be done well in a hurry.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an optician, an optometrist and an ophthalmologist?

An ophthalmologist is a medical doctor who diagnoses and treats eye diseases. An optician makes glasses and fits contact lenses; an optometrist is an optician with higher training, specialised in eye examinations. To have your eyesight measured you can go directly to a federally certified optician; if anything calls for a medical check, the practice refers you to an ophthalmologist.

What does “Swiss federally certified optician (master optician)” mean?

It is the higher federal diploma of the optician’s profession in Switzerland. Beyond basic training it covers eye examinations and contact lens fitting. Marco Rusca obtained it in 1986 at the Swiss Higher School of Optics in Olten and has practised the profession for more than forty years.

How often should I have my eyes tested?

If you have no complaints, about every two years. Sooner if you notice changes: difficulty reading, headaches, trouble driving at night, tiredness in front of a screen.

Why can’t I get used to my progressive lenses?
